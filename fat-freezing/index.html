What is fat freezing?

Fat freezing, also called cryolipolysis, is a non-invasive body contouring treatment built around controlled cooling. Fat cells are more sensitive to cold than the skin and muscle around them. Cooled fat cells undergo programmed cell death, and the body's immune and lymphatic systems clear them naturally over the following weeks.

It's about shape, not the number on the scales. B Sculpt isn't a weight-loss clinic, and fat freezing is not a substitute for a healthy lifestyle, diet or exercise. If weight loss is your goal, your GP is the best person to talk to.

How a Cold session works

  1. Cups matched to the area. Applicator cups come in different sizes, chosen for the area being treated. A protective gel pad sits between your skin and the cup.
  2. Controlled cooling of the fat layer. The cup draws the area in gently and cools it. Cooling is set and monitored throughout.
  3. Cooling, applied precisely. Each applicator cools the targeted area while the surrounding tissue is left alone. Up to four applicators can be used at once.
  4. Over the following weeks. Cooled fat cells break down and are cleared gradually through the body's normal processes. Results vary between individuals.

Sessions take under an hour, and you can relax while the treatment works.

Areas we treat with fat freezing

Larger cups are used for the abdomen, flanks and thighs, and smaller cups for the arms and smaller areas. Sonya recommends which areas suit you at your consultation, based on your goals and your health.

Who fat freezing is for

Cold is for men and women aged 18 and over who want to reduce a stubborn area of fat. Many clients come to us after weight loss or after having a baby.

Not everyone is suitable. Fat freezing must not be performed if you have cryoglobulinemia, cold agglutinin disease, paroxysmal cold hemoglobinuria or cold urticaria, if you are pregnant or breastfeeding, or if you have a hernia or compromised skin in or near the treatment area. Other conditions, such as Raynaud's phenomenon, need to be assessed case by case. Sonya goes through a full health screening at your consultation, and treatment only goes ahead if it's suitable for you.

What it feels like, side effects and downtime

Expect strong cold and a pulling sensation at first, easing as the area numbs.

There is usually no downtime. The treated area usually feels cold and numb for a while afterwards, and may be a little red or tender for a few days. Common, usually temporary, side effects include redness, swelling or bruising, numbness or tingling, tenderness, and mild itching.

Less common risks include prolonged numbness, contour irregularities and, rarely, paradoxical adipose hyperplasia (PAH), where the treated fat increases in volume instead of decreasing. Sonya talks you through possible side effects before your first treatment. Read the full risks, side effects & aftercare.

When you may notice changes

Changes build gradually. After a Cold treatment, the body clears the treated fat cells over the following weeks to months. Most people do a course of sessions, and Sonya records measurements at each visit so you can see how you're going.

Results vary between individuals and are not guaranteed. A consultation determines whether fat freezing is suitable for you, and what a sensible course looks like.

Fat freezing: frequent questions

Yes. Cryolipolysis is the clinical name and fat freezing is the everyday name. At B Sculpt, the treatment is called Cold, and we use the CoolSlimming Ultimate 360.

Is fat freezing a weight-loss treatment?

No. B Sculpt isn't a weight-loss clinic. Fat freezing cools the fat layer in a targeted area to change its shape, and it's not a substitute for a healthy lifestyle, diet or exercise. If weight loss is your goal, your GP is the best person to talk to.

What does fat freezing feel like?

Expect strong cold and a pulling sensation at first, easing as the area numbs. Redness and tenderness afterwards are common.

Is there any downtime after fat freezing?

There is usually no downtime. The treated area usually feels cold and numb for a while afterwards, and may be a little red or tender for a few days.
